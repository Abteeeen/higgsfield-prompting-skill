## Nine original options

### Emotional storytelling

**E1 — The Last Light.** A tiny firefly is the light inside a streetlamp. A lost child passes beneath it; the firefly leaves the lamp to guide the child home, becoming dimmer along the way. At the doorstep the child makes a small sheltered resting place for it. The street is dark, but the two are safe. Working length: 40–50 seconds. Opening image: a tired firefly struggling to keep a huge lamp glowing. Challenge: preserve the size relationship, the child's identity and the firefly's changing brightness. This develops the existing unapproved concept; it does not inherit approval from the earlier rejected generation attempt.

**E2 — The Tide Keeper.** A tiny crab turns a wheel that raises a miniature harbor bridge for passing boats. A storm traps a small boat on the wrong side. The crab cannot move the wheel alone; the boat's passengers disembark and help, then give the crab a proper dry shelter beside its workplace. Working length: 40–50 seconds. Opening image: the camera reveals that the enormous bridge depends on a crab-sized wheel. Challenge: readable geography and coordinated movement. The solution is collective help, not a sacrifice or copied family-rescue plot.

**E3 — The Cloud Repairer.** A small worker stitches a tear in a raincloud above a dry garden. The repaired cloud waters the seedlings but leaves the worker soaked. The gardener discovers the worker and uses a broad leaf as an umbrella; a new plant grows where they sit. Working length: 35–45 seconds. Opening image: a needle pulls thread through the sky. Challenge: establish the physical rules quickly and keep water, thread and scale understandable without explanatory dialogue.

### Impossible everyday companions

**C1 — The Loyal Raincloud.** A person treats a small raincloud like a pet, hanging its lead beside the door and taking it on an errand. It causes awkward puddles, then spots a wilting flower outside a closed shop and waters only that pot. The person waits proudly rather than pulling it away. Working length: 35–40 seconds. Opening image: a leash disappears upward into a tiny indoor cloud. Challenge: make the cloud expressive through movement and rain without an unstable human face. The final useful act gives the sequence a payoff beyond several unrelated surreal images.

**C2 — The Snail Courier.** A giant snail carries a person's groceries through a narrow street. Its slow arrival frustrates the waiting household, but the shell opens to reveal that the fragile birthday cake survived perfectly while hurried deliveries nearby are battered. Working length: 35–40 seconds. Opening image: a shopping bag hangs from a shell larger than the doorway. Challenge: maintain shell scale, cargo placement and the cake reveal. The snail's slowness must be clear through editing rather than long empty holds.

**C3 — The Laundry Whale.** A small floating whale accompanies someone doing laundry. It keeps swallowing stray socks, apparently making the task worse. At the end it exhales neatly matched pairs onto the drying rack. Working length: 35–40 seconds. Opening image: a whale emerges from a laundry basket with one sock on its tail. Challenge: object permanence, mouth interactions and convincing sock pairs; likely more repair work than the cloud concept.

### Physical comedy with fictional characters

**K1 — The Goal That Wouldn't Stay.** A neighborhood mascot prepares an elaborate penalty kick. Before every kick, the portable goal rolls a little farther away. The goalkeeper finally plants it firmly; the mascot celebrates early, taps the ball, and it stops just short. Working length: 35–40 seconds. Opening image: a completely open goal quietly rolling away behind the celebrating kicker. Challenge: ball-ground contact and consistent goal geometry. The final failure has a different cause from the setup, giving the escalation a turn.

**K2 — The Overprepared Goalie.** A robot goalkeeper equips itself with increasingly absurd protective gear for a tiny child's shot. The equipment makes it too wide to move. The child calmly rolls the ball through the gap between its boots and offers it a comforting sticker afterward. Working length: 35–40 seconds. Opening image: a robot installs an enormous shield while a tiny ball waits on the spot. Challenge: stable robot design and cumulative equipment continuity. Use invented characters and a simple playground rather than public figures or a professional club.

**K3 — The Celebration Rehearsal.** An amateur player rehearses a complicated goal celebration before taking a shot. A dog wanders through, nudges the ball into the net and copies one simple celebratory movement. Everyone cheers the dog while the player is still rehearsing. Working length: 35–40 seconds. Opening image: a player practicing a back-to-camera victory pose beside an untouched ball. Challenge: animal motion and readable eyelines; the dog should perform one plausible movement, not a long human dance.

## Recommended discussion order

These judgments concern clarity and production complexity, not predicted views.

| Paper finalist | Why keep it | What would make us reject or revise it |
|---|---|---|
| **C1 — The Loyal Raincloud** | A recognizable errand, one main person and a simple companion silhouette; useful for evaluating whether the surreal-companion direction suits the account | The opening requires text to explain it, the leash/cloud relation is unreadable, or the payoff feels like a disconnected extra scene |
| **E1 — The Last Light** | Strong visual contrast and a clear relationship; the closest match to the strongest observed narrative mechanism without copying its plot | The firefly's exhaustion is unreadable, the child appears abruptly, or the ending needs narration to explain the kindness |
| **K2 — The Overprepared Goalie** | Clear visual irony and an ending that remains understandable without celebrity recognition | Equipment changes break continuity, the ball path cannot be read, or the comedy depends on dialogue rather than action |

My provisional preference for a first **technical test after authorization** is C1 because it has fewer interacting limbs and props than the other finalists. My preference for deeper story development is E1. Neither preference selects the account's niche or predicts a higher payout. The user has not selected a concept.

## Paper review before any production

For each finalist, make a six-beat text outline: first visible surprise, relationship, problem, attempted solution, reversal and ending. Each beat must change the situation. Remove beats that merely repeat the same spectacle. Check that a viewer can identify the subject, location and consequence from the described images alone.

Then specify the minimum asset list and the hardest interaction. A future Higgsfield test should examine that interaction, not render a complete film prematurely. Keep the appearance references and settings fixed while changing one motion instruction. A separate full-film plan would follow only after the user chooses the direction and authorizes generation.

### C1 paper outline

1. A person clips a lead onto a collar floating beneath a small cloud in the hallway.
2. The cloud follows the person outside, keeping pace like a pet.
3. A short shower leaves a puddle on the person's shoes; the person looks up, unimpressed.
4. The cloud stops beside a wilting potted flower; the person initially tries to continue walking.
5. A narrow shower waters the pot while the person stays dry. The person notices the purpose and waits.
6. The person sets down the shopping bag and sits beside the cloud and watered plant. The ending shows acceptance, without requiring implausibly instant plant growth.

Minimum assets: one person/outfit reference, one cloud silhouette reference, hallway and shopfront references, one distinctive flowerpot. Hardest interaction: the cloud staying above the pot while its rain misses the adjacent person. A later technical comparison could change only broad rain direction versus an explicit pot-centered rain area; both versions would use the same composition. Check the full clip for cloud drift, rain landing location and unwanted changes to the person.

### E1 paper outline

1. Inside a streetlamp, the small firefly braces itself against the glass as its light flickers.
2. Outside, a child stops at an unfamiliar fork in a dark lane and looks around.
3. The firefly sees the child, leaving the lamp dark when it slips through a gap.
4. It flies ahead in short bursts; the child follows from one visible landmark to the next.
5. At a familiar doorstep the child reaches safety, but the firefly can barely stay aloft.
6. The child places a small cloth beside the sheltered doorstep. The firefly settles; a final gentle glow illuminates the child's relieved face.

Minimum assets: firefly appearance/scale reference, child/outfit reference, streetlamp, lane layout and doorstep. Hardest interaction: a tiny moving light guiding a consistently sized child through darkness. A later test could compare a broad instruction to follow the firefly with explicit lead/follower positions. Check scale, screen direction, visibility and whether the light source remains the same creature. The test would not establish whether the whole story is emotionally effective.

### K2 paper outline

1. A small child waits beside a tiny football while a robot goalkeeper unfolds an oversized shield.
2. The robot theatrically signals readiness; the child takes one quiet step back.
3. The robot adds a helmet and shoulder panels, making the task look more dangerous than it is.
4. It attempts a sideways warm-up and discovers the equipment jams against the goalposts.
5. The child rolls the ball between the robot's widely planted boots; the robot cannot bend down in time.
6. The child places a cheerful sticker on the motionless shield. The robot lowers its head toward the sticker rather than reacting angrily.

Minimum assets: robot with three clearly specified equipment states, child/outfit, ball and fixed goal layout. Hardest interaction: ball passage through the gap between stationary boots. A later test could compare an ambiguous scoring instruction with explicit start position, path and end position. Keep camera and geometry fixed, and reject clips where the ball passes through a boot, duplicates or disappears.
