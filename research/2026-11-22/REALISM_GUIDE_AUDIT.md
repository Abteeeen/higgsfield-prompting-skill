## What the guide actually teaches

The workflow first prepares people, locations and recurring props, then assigns actions and camera coverage. Mika's initial sheet contains heads in the outfit panels; the edited sheet uses neck-down views. The Italy pair changes the table from two settings to three. Distinct apartment seats and nearby landmarks make a camera-angle change easier to describe without moving the characters. These are useful preparation examples, not proof of exact identity preservation.

The phone provides a clear cause for travel and a visible handoff between characters. The first transition matches the person across a location cut; the second uses a foreground shoulder/back as a wipe. The Japan diagram separates seating and tableware from appearance. It shows three personal plates, two shared plates and an empty fourth side. The final product shot introduces packaging before revealing the cat, connecting the ending to an opening gag.

The article explicitly says its public recreation prompts are **adapted text-only versions**, while the displayed media comes from the original project. It does not promise that the text alone reproduces the cast or take. That disclosure matters more than superficial prompt/result similarity.
