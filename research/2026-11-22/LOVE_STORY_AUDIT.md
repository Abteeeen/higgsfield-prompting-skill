## Useful structure

The story repeats an encounter, obstacle and missed connection across harbor, arena, railway, pirate, carnival and elevator settings. Preparation separates recurring faces from era-specific wardrobe, locations and scale references. The final instructions explicitly call for batches, take selection and editing. This is a multi-stage production plan.

Useful conventions include giving each character an immediate goal, letting reactions follow visible events, specifying listener behavior, and assigning references only the attributes they should supply. The carnival-to-elevator transition uses a recurring hand gesture and a sound bridge. Those are editorial ideas to develop in original stories; identity locks, exact timing and commercial-success claims remain untested.

The same shot plan should settle these issues before more descriptive detail is added. A long prompt does not resolve incompatible instructions.

The audio reference is a **7.168-second, 48kHz mono MP3**. Whole-file Whisper transcription produces an implausibly long repetition of a sung syllable, not a reliable spoken transcript. Do not turn that ASR output into scripted dialogue or claim that audio timing has been validated against a finished film. No finished film is exposed in the article's video elements.

For our workflow, approve one asset version and one intended state per scene before prompting: costume, prop ownership, scale and movement direction. A reference image can conflict with a numerical instruction just as two prose instructions can conflict with each other.
