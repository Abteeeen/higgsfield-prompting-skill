## Question

Can a visible firefly lead a child through a simple scene while preserving their appearance, relative size and order? This is the difficult guiding interaction in [The Last Light](../../CONCEPT_OPTIONS.md#e1-paper-outline). A successful clip would establish feasibility for this setup only. It would not validate the complete story, viewer interest, reliable generation or Dreamina eligibility.

The starting composition should show one child in a yellow raincoat and red boots on the left, facing right; one amber firefly ahead at shoulder height; a cobbled lane and a fence post farther right. Leave enough room for four small steps. Use enough ambient light to see the child and enough on-screen size to distinguish the firefly from an anonymous glowing dot. The firefly must still look small relative to the child. Approve that visual compromise before animation.

Lock one appearance and prop state for this scene. No rabbit or additional carried prop is needed in the current six-beat outline. If the story is revised to require one, update the approved reference and continuity record before testing. The firefly's appearance, brightness and the surroundings should remain steady here; exhaustion and lamp departure are separate later shots.

## Copyable prompt draft

The reference supplies the approved appearance and starting positions. Assign it through the actual interface; the following prose contains no invented reference handles.

```text
One continuous fixed side view of a quiet cobbled lane at night. Animate the approved starting composition. One child in a yellow raincoat and red boots walks from left to right toward the fence post. One small amber firefly flies ahead at the child's shoulder height, its body and wings distinguishable within its steady warm glow. Cool ambient light keeps both subjects readable.

The child follows the firefly for four small steps. Both slow and stop before reaching the fence post. Hold that final arrangement briefly. Keep the same child, clothing, firefly appearance and relative sizes throughout. Preserve the lane and fence geometry. No cuts, camera movement, extra characters, readable text or subtitles.
```

This is **variant A**, a broad follow instruction. For **variant B**, replace only the sentence “The child follows the firefly for four small steps.” with:

```text
The firefly stays roughly one child's arm-length ahead on the right as the child takes four small steps along the same path; neither overtakes the other, and their gap stays approximately steady.
```

Keep the reference files, their bindings, all other words and every available generation control unchanged. Record a seed only if the actual interface exposes one; do not invent seed control or claim deterministic comparison. Keep both outputs and their attempt order. One pair can suggest which wording to investigate, but generation variability prevents treating it as proof that the changed sentence caused the difference.

## Review every complete output

| Check | Pass target | Failure that matters to the story |
|---|---|---|
| Readable guide | The firefly remains visibly the same creature, ahead of the child | It becomes a featureless dot, disappears or changes into another object |
| Following action | Both travel rightward in the same scene; the child takes visible steps | Teleporting, reversed order, overtaking or a stationary child |
| Relative scale and gap | Similar apparent body sizes and a readable lead/follower distance | Large size changes or a gap that makes the guiding relationship unclear |
| Identity and continuity | Child, clothing, firefly and lane remain recognizable throughout | Outfit transfer, duplicate subjects, shifting fence geometry or an abrupt scene change |
| Ending | Both slow and stop before the same fence post, with a brief readable hold | Continued travel off-screen, an unexplained cut or a missing final state |

Watch normal-speed motion first, then inspect questionable intervals and scene boundaries more closely. Record what actually happened, with timestamps, rather than copying the intended actions into the result column. Inspect dimensions and duration from the actual file. Do not call a timeline contact sheet continuous playback.

If neither variant makes the guide readable, revise composition, lighting or scale as a new comparison; adding more prompt adjectives does not resolve an invisible subject. If appearance drifts, revisit the reference setup. If following fails while appearance is stable, simplify the action or refine blocking. If the interaction works, return to the full text storyboard and decide the next test with the user; this brief does not authorize full production.
